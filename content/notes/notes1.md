---
title: "On building in public and staying sane"
date: "2025-05-01"
tag: "life"
---

Building in public is one of those ideas that sounds simple but gets complicated fast. You share your work, people respond, and ideally you grow an audience.

The part nobody talks about is the emotional overhead. Every update is a small act of vulnerability. The project that felt exciting in private suddenly feels inadequate when you imagine strangers looking at it.

I've found two things help:

1. **Ship before you're ready.** Not recklessly — but don't let polish become a reason to hide. Rough edges are fine. Unfinished is fine.

2. **Define what "in public" means for you.** You don't have to post every commit. A weekly update, a short thread, a changelog — pick a format you can sustain.

The goal isn't to perform progress. It's to stay accountable to yourself while building something worth sharing.
